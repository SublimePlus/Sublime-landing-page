Everyone wants the viral moment. The post that explodes overnight, the video with millions of views, the tweet everyone screenshots. It's exciting, and when it happens, it feels like you've cracked the code.

Then a week later, everything goes back to normal. The followers who came for one funny post drift away. The spike flattens. And you're left trying to figure out how to do it again.

At Sublime, we've come to a strong opinion on this. Going viral is overrated. Consistency isn't.

## Viral is a lottery ticket

Nobody can reliably make something go viral. Not the biggest brands, not the best creators. It depends on timing, luck, algorithms and a hundred things nobody controls.

Building a marketing plan around viral moments is like building a retirement plan around lottery tickets. It might happen. It probably won't.

## Familiarity builds liking

There's a well studied idea in psychology called the mere exposure effect. Put simply, people tend to like things more the more often they encounter them. A [post from Colby College's cognitive science blog](https://web.colby.edu/cogblog/?p=6725) explains how repeated exposure to something, like a song or an advertisement, can increase how much we like it.

For brands, that's a quiet superpower. Showing up regularly, in the right places, makes people more comfortable with you over time. Not through one big moment, but through many small ones.

## Consistency is what AI tools notice

This goes beyond human psychology. AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews build their picture of a brand from patterns across the web. One viral post is a single data point. Steady mentions over months become a pattern.

That's a big reason why the brands that show up again and again are the ones that get recommended. I wrote more about that in [why ranking on ChatGPT matters more every month](/blog/why-ranking-on-chatgpt-matters-more-every-month).

## Communities reward people who stick around

Reddit is a good example. The users people trust aren't the ones who posted one hit. They're the ones who've been answering questions and helping out for months. The same goes for brands in any community. Showing up once is noise. Showing up consistently is reputation. [How Reddit threads rank on Google](/blog/how-reddit-threads-rank-on-google) shows how that steady presence can pay off in search too.

## What consistency looks like

It's not glamorous. A regular posting rhythm you can actually keep up. A recognizable voice and look. Useful content, week after week. Replies to comments and reviews. Showing up in the same communities over and over.

None of it will trend. All of it adds up.

## If you do go viral

Enjoy it. Then go right back to being consistent. A viral moment is most valuable when there's a steady brand underneath to catch the people who arrive.
