For years, brand voice was about one audience: people. How does your website sound to a customer? Does your Instagram caption feel friendly or stiff? Does your email newsletter sound like the same company as your packaging?

Those questions still matter. But there's a new listener in the room now. AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews are reading everything your brand says and everything said about it. And then they repeat it to other people.

At Sublime+, we think this changes what brand voice is for.

## AI tools are paraphrasers

When someone asks ChatGPT "what does this company do?" or "is this brand any good?", the answer is built from what the AI has read. It paraphrases your website, your reviews, your social profiles and the conversations around you.

If all of those describe you the same way, the AI's description will be clear and accurate. If they're all over the place, the AI has to guess. And guesses can be wrong.

## More people are hearing that paraphrase

This isn't a small audience. [Pew Research Center found](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/25/34-of-us-adults-have-used-chatgpt-about-double-the-share-in-2023/) that 34% of US adults had used ChatGPT by early 2025, roughly double the share in 2023. For a growing number of people, the first description they hear of your brand might come from an AI, not from you.

## Inconsistency is the real enemy

Most brands don't have a bad voice. They have several voices. The homepage says one thing, the LinkedIn page says another, the Google Business Profile is three years old and the founder describes the company differently on every podcast.

To a human, that's a little messy. To an AI, it's confusing. Confusion shows up as vague answers, missing details or mix ups with other companies. If that's already happening to you, [why ChatGPT says something wrong about your brand](/blog/chatgpt-wrong-about-your-brand) explains why.

## The words other people use matter too

Your voice isn't only what you say. It's also the words customers use about you. If reviews keep calling you "the friendly option" or "the one with great support," those phrases often end up in AI answers.

You can't script what customers say. But you can give them a clear, memorable experience that's easy to describe.

## A simple exercise

Write one plain sentence that says what your brand does and who it's for. Then check whether your website, social bios, review profiles and directory listings all say roughly the same thing. If they don't, start there.
