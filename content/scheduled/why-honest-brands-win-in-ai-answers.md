There used to be a lot of places for a brand to hide. A bad review on page three. A complaint thread nobody found. A product page that quietly left out the downsides.

AI tools changed that. ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews read widely and summarize what they find. The good, the bad and the awkward. At Sublime, we've come to a simple conclusion: honesty isn't just a nice value anymore. It's a practical advantage.

## AI tools read the whole story

When someone asks an AI tool about your brand, it doesn't only read your homepage. It reads reviews, Reddit threads, comparison articles and news. If there's a pattern of complaints, it notices. If there's a pattern of praise, it notices that too.

You can't polish your way out of a pattern. You can only change what the pattern is made of.

## Fake shortcuts are getting riskier

Some brands try to fix their image with fake reviews or paid praise. That's become a much worse idea. The US Federal Trade Commission [announced a final rule](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) that bans fake reviews and testimonials, including AI generated fake reviews, along with buying reviews and selling or buying fake followers and views.

Beyond the legal risk, fake signals tend to get spotted. Platforms remove them, communities call them out, and once people catch a brand faking it, that story becomes the thing they remember.

## Imperfect is believable

Here's the part that surprises a lot of founders. A perfect record isn't what earns trust. A whitepaper from [Northwestern University's Spiegel Research Center](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/04/Online-Reviews-Whitepaper.pdf) found that purchase likelihood peaks for products rated a bit below a perfect five stars, and that negative reviews help establish trust.

People expect a few bumps. What they watch is how you handle them.

## Honesty compounds

When a brand is upfront about what it does well and what it doesn't, something nice happens. The right customers find it, the wrong ones skip it, and the reviews that follow are more positive because expectations were set correctly.

Over time, those honest reviews and conversations become the material AI tools learn from. So the answer they give about you gets more accurate and more favorable at the same time.

## What honesty looks like in practice

It's not complicated. Describe your product accurately, including who it's not for. Reply to criticism calmly and fix what's actually broken. Let real customers speak in their own words. And when something is wrong online, correct it with facts rather than spin.
