When someone asks ChatGPT for "a good Italian restaurant near downtown Denver" or asks Gemini "which plumber in Leeds actually shows up on time," the answer has to come from somewhere. A big part of it usually comes from reviews. And for local businesses, the reviews that matter most are usually the ones on Google.

So those stars under your Google Business Profile aren't just for people scrolling Maps anymore. They're shaping what AI tools say about you.

## Why Google reviews carry so much weight

Google reviews are everywhere. They sit on Google Maps, in local search results and on your Business Profile, and there are a lot of them. For many local businesses, Google holds more reviews than every other platform combined.

That volume makes them a rich source for anything trying to sum up your reputation. Google's own AI features, including AI Overviews and Gemini, sit right on top of that data. Other AI tools that search the web can find it too, along with Yelp, Tripadvisor and other review sites.

## What AI tools actually pick up

It's not just your star rating. AI tools read the words.

If dozens of reviews mention "fast service," "great with kids" or "fair prices," those phrases become part of how you're described. If several mention "rude staff" or "hidden fees," that sticks too. AI tools are good at spotting patterns across many reviews, so one recurring complaint can follow you into answers even when your average rating looks fine.

Recency matters as well. Reviews from last month say more about you today than reviews from three years ago.

## How to make your reviews work for you

**Keep your Business Profile complete.** Correct hours, categories, services, photos and a clear description. AI tools pull these details along with the reviews.

**Ask for reviews steadily.** A few new reviews every week beats one big push a year. Ask right after a good experience, when people are happiest. A QR code at the counter or a link in your follow up message makes it easy.

**Let customers use their own words.** Don't script what they write. Natural reviews that mention specific things, like a dish, a service or a staff member's name, give AI tools much richer material.

**Reply to as many reviews as you can.** Thank the happy ones and answer the unhappy ones calmly. Your replies are public and become part of the picture. I covered the tough ones in [how to deal with unfair bad reviews without looking defensive](/blog/deal-with-unfair-bad-reviews).

**Fix what keeps coming up.** If the same complaint appears again and again, that's not a review problem. It's a business problem, and solving it is the fastest way to change what AI tools say.

## What not to do

Don't buy reviews, don't offer discounts in exchange for five star ratings, and don't ask only the customers you know are happy while quietly skipping everyone else. Google's policies prohibit these practices, and in the US the FTC rule on fake reviews covers some of them too. Getting caught can mean removed reviews, a warning on your profile or worse.

## Google reviews are one piece of the puzzle

AI tools also read Reddit, local forums, news and your own website. The business with strong Google reviews and real conversations happening about it elsewhere is the one that gets recommended most.
