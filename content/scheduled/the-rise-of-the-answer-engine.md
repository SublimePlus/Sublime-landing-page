For about twenty five years, we've used search engines. You type something in, you get a list of links, and the work of finding the answer is still yours.

Now we're living through the rise of the answer engine. You ask a question, and the tool does the reading for you and hands back an answer. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Google's AI Overviews all work this way. At Sublime, we think this is one of the biggest shifts in how people find information since the search engine itself.

## Search engine vs answer engine

A search engine finds pages. An answer engine finds answers.

That sounds like a small difference, but it changes everything about the experience. With a search engine, you compare sources yourself. With an answer engine, the comparing happens before you even see the result.

## People are already relying on them

On Google itself, [Pew Research Center found](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) that around one in five searches in March 2025 produced an AI summary, and people clicked traditional result links less often when one appeared. Outside Google, a separate [Pew survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/25/34-of-us-adults-have-used-chatgpt-about-double-the-share-in-2023/) found that 34% of US adults had used ChatGPT by early 2025, roughly double the share in 2023.

The answer engine isn't coming. It's here.

## What is answer engine optimization?

Answer engine optimization, often shortened to AEO, is the work of making your brand and your content show up inside those answers. You'll also hear it called GEO, LLM SEO, AI search optimization or LLM marketing. The names overlap, and honestly, the industry hasn't settled on one yet.

Whatever you call it, the goal is the same: when someone asks a question your business can answer, your brand is part of the response. I explained the idea in plain terms in [what is LLM marketing](/blog/what-is-llm-marketing).

## What answer engines seem to like

From what we've seen, answer engines tend to lean on a few things.

**Clear answers.** Content that answers a question directly and plainly is easier to use.

**Real experience.** Reviews, community discussions and firsthand accounts give answers credibility.

**Consistency.** Brands described the same way across many sources are easier to recognize and recommend.

**Trusted sources.** Established sites, active communities and reputable review platforms carry more weight than random pages.

## What this means for brands

The question shifts from "how do we rank?" to "how do we become the answer?" That means thinking beyond your own website, toward everywhere your brand gets discussed.

It also means accepting that you can't buy your way into an organic answer. You earn it by being the brand people genuinely talk about. I made that case in [whoever owns AI answers owns the future of marketing](/blog/future-of-marketing-ai-answers).
