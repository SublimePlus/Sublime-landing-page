People ask about our name more than you'd think. It usually comes up in the first five minutes of a call. "So why Sublime?" Sometimes it's "wait, is it Sublime, Sublime Plus or Sublime+?" The answer is yes, all three. More on that in a minute.

This post is the story behind the name, and why it fits the kind of marketing we believe in.

## It took a lot of meetings

Picking the name wasn't quick. My cofounder and I sat through long meetings, filled pages with options and talked each one through. We didn't want a label. We wanted a word with real meaning behind it, something we could work toward every day. A name that would push us to do better work just by saying it out loud.

## What "sublime" actually means

The word comes from the Latin sublimis, which roughly means lifted up or lofty. In everyday English, sublime describes something so good it stops you for a second. A view from the top of a mountain. A perfect plate of food. A song you replay five times in a row.

That's a big word for a marketing agency, and we know it. But that was the point. A name like Sublime sets a standard, and it reminds us of that standard every time we write it on a proposal.

## The names that didn't make it

We went through plenty of candidates. Subliminal was a serious one for a while. It sounds great and it's catchy. But subliminal messaging isn't exactly what you want a marketing agency to be known for, so it didn't survive the meeting.

Nothing else came close to Sublime. It's easy on the tongue, easy to remember and it means something positive. That combination is rarer than you'd think.

## And the plus?

The plus has two meanings for us.

The first is the little extra. If you've been to our homepage, you've seen the line "done with a little extra." For us that means caring about the details most people skip. How a brand sounds when someone reads about it in a Reddit thread. Whether an AI tool describes a client correctly. Small things, but they add up.

The second is about the future. We see Sublime+ as an umbrella. Today it covers our marketing work on Reddit, SEO, AI visibility and visual content. Over time, we'd like to build more under that same name. The plus leaves room to grow.

## Sublime, Sublime Plus, Sublime+ or sublime-plus?

All of them. You'll see us write Sublime Plus in full, Sublime+ when space is tight and plain Sublime when we're talking casually. Our website lives at sublime-plus.com, because domain names don't do plus signs. Some people find us by searching Sublime marketing or Sublime Plus Marketing, and that's us too.

One small note: the word sublime is popular. There's a well known code editor, and a few other companies use it in their names. We're not related to any of them. We're the Sublime that helps brands get found on Google, discussed on Reddit and recommended inside AI answers.

## Why naming matters more in the age of AI

There's a funny thing about naming a company today. You're not only naming it for people anymore. You're naming it for ChatGPT, Gemini and Google too.

AI tools learn who a brand is from how consistently it's described across the web. A name that's shared with other companies needs extra care, so we try to always pair ours with what we actually do. I wrote about how name confusion affects brands in [your competitor shows up in ChatGPT and you don't](/blog/competitor-shows-up-in-chatgpt).

## What clients say about it

Some of our clients ask about the name on one of our first calls. When we explain what it means and how we landed on it, the reaction is usually warm. People like that there's a real story behind it, not just a word that sounded cool on a logo.

That's when I realized a name with meaning does more than identify you. It gives people something to remember you by.

## What we want the name to stand for

A name is a promise you make before anyone has met you. Ours is a little ambitious. What we want is simple: that the people we work with feel they got the service they deserved, and a little more than they expected. That's the sublime part and the plus part, together.

Some days we nail it. Some days we learn something. Either way, it's a good thing to have written above the door.
