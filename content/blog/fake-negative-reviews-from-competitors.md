Picture this. Your Google reviews have been steady for months. Then over two days, six one star reviews land. No order numbers you recognize. Vague complaints. A couple of them mention a competitor by name as the "much better option."

It's maddening, and sadly it happens. Some businesses try to drag down a rival's rating instead of improving their own.

We hear about this at Sublime Plus more than you'd expect, so this is what you can actually do about it, and what you shouldn't.

## How to tell if reviews might be fake

You can rarely prove it right away, but certain patterns are worth noticing.

Several negative reviews arrive in a short burst after a quiet stretch. The reviewers have no matching purchase, booking or conversation in your records. The accounts are brand new, or they've only ever left one review. The complaints are generic and could describe any business. Or the reviews go out of their way to praise one specific competitor.

One of these on its own doesn't mean much. Real customers have bad days too. A cluster of them is when you should start paying attention.

## Document everything first

Before you reply or report anything, take screenshots of every review, every reviewer profile and the dates. Search your records for each name. Write down any pattern you notice.

That record matters when you report the reviews, and it matters even more if things escalate later.

## Report through the platform

Google, Yelp, Trustpilot and most other review sites let you flag reviews that break their rules. Fake reviews, conflicts of interest and reviews from people who were never customers are against the guidelines almost everywhere.

Use the official reporting tools and include your evidence. Keep it factual. Removal isn't instant and isn't guaranteed, but a clear, documented report has a much better chance.

## Reply publicly, without accusing anyone

While you wait, post a calm reply under each review. Don't call it fake and don't name the competitor, even if you're sure. Readers can't see your evidence, and an accusation makes you look defensive.

Something simple works: *"We can't find any record of an order under this name, and we'd really like to understand what happened. Please reach out to us directly so we can look into it."* Future readers see a business that cares. If the reviewer never replies, that silence says plenty on its own.

I wrote more about tone in [how to deal with unfair bad reviews without looking defensive](/blog/deal-with-unfair-bad-reviews).

## Know your options if it keeps happening

Fake reviews are illegal in many places, and in the US the FTC has a rule that targets them. If the attacks are serious and ongoing, and your evidence points to a specific company, it may be worth talking to a lawyer about your options. I'm not a lawyer, so treat that as a starting point rather than advice.

## Never fight back the same way

It's tempting to hit back with fake reviews of your own, either praising yourself or attacking them. Don't. Platforms catch it, it can get your profile flagged, and it puts you on the wrong side of the same rules you're hoping they'll enforce.

## Build a wall of real reviews

The strongest protection is volume. Six fake reviews hurt a lot when you only have twenty real ones. They barely register when you have three hundred.

So ask happy customers to share their experience, and keep asking. Reviews also reach further than your profile page now.
