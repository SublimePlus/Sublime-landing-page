A few years ago, the idea of an influencer who doesn't exist sounded like science fiction. Now AI influencers, virtual characters created with AI who post, pose and talk like real creators, are showing up all over social media.

Some people find it exciting. Some find it a little unsettling. At Sublime Plus, we work with AI brand ambassadors, so we've had plenty of conversations about both reactions. This is my honest take.

## What an AI influencer actually is

An AI influencer is a digital character with a consistent look, personality and voice. They can appear in photos and videos, wear products, visit places and share opinions, all without a real person in front of the camera.

Some are clearly fictional and lean into it. Others look so realistic that viewers aren't sure what they're seeing.

## Why brands are curious

The appeal is easy to understand.

**Control.** The character always looks right, sounds on brand and shows up on time.

**Consistency.** No scheduling conflicts, no travel, no bad days.

**Creative freedom.** The same character can be in Tokyo in the morning and on a mountain by lunch.

**No surprises.** A virtual creator won't suddenly post something that embarrasses the brand.

## The honest questions to ask first

This is where it gets interesting, and where brands need to be careful.

**Is it clear the character is AI?** Audiences tend to feel tricked when they find out later. Being upfront usually goes over better than a big reveal.

**What is the character claiming?** The US Federal Trade Commission's guidance for influencers, [Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/disclosures-101-new-ftc-resources-social-media-influencers), says influencers should disclose brand relationships clearly and shouldn't talk about experiences with products they haven't actually tried. A virtual character can't truly try anything, so it shouldn't pretend to have personal experiences or give fake testimonials.

**Are the numbers real?** The FTC's [final rule on reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) also bans buying or selling fake followers and views. A virtual creator still needs a real audience.

## Where AI influencers make sense

They shine as brand mascots, fictional characters, product showcases and storytellers. Think of them as the modern version of a brand character, with much more range.

They make less sense when a brand needs genuine human experience, like a skincare routine that worked on real skin or an honest review of a service. That's where real customers and real creators still matter most.

## My take

AI influencers aren't a fad, and they're not a replacement for people either. Used honestly, they're a powerful creative tool. Used deceptively, they're a trust problem waiting to happen. The difference comes down to transparency.
