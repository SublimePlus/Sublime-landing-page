Picture a Saturday afternoon about twenty years ago. You need new sneakers. You drive to the mall, wander into three stores, try on a few pairs, ask the salesperson what's popular and go home with whatever felt right.

Now picture the same need today. You might open ChatGPT and type "best sneakers for walking all day under $120." A few seconds later you have a short list, a reason for each pick and maybe a link or two. You never left the couch.

That gap, from the mall to a chat window, is one of the biggest changes in how people buy things. It's also why we built Sublime Plus as a marketing agency focused on where buying decisions happen now. So let me walk through how we got here.

## Era one: walk around and ask

For a long time, buying meant physically going somewhere. The store decided what you saw. The salesperson was your main source of advice, along with friends and family.

Brands fought for shelf space and good placement. If you weren't on the shelf, you barely existed.

## Era two: search, compare, repeat

Then came search engines and online shopping. Suddenly you could compare ten products in ten tabs, read reviews from strangers and order from your phone.

Online shopping has kept growing steadily ever since. According to the [US Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/retail/ecommerce.html), ecommerce made up 16.4% of all US retail sales in 2025, up from 16.1% the year before.

Brands now fought for a different kind of shelf: the first page of Google.

## Era three: just ask

Now we're in the third era. Instead of comparing ten tabs, more people hand that job to an AI tool and ask for a recommendation.

The shift is happening fast. [Pew Research Center found](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/06/25/34-of-us-adults-have-used-chatgpt-about-double-the-share-in-2023/) that 34% of US adults had used ChatGPT by early 2025, roughly double the share in 2023. And ChatGPT is just one option, alongside Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and the AI Overviews at the top of Google.

The AI reads the reviews, the forums and the comparison articles for you, then hands you its short list.

## What stayed exactly the same

Through all three eras, people wanted the same thing: a trusted voice to help them feel confident about a choice.

At the mall, that was the salesperson or a friend. In the search era, it was reviews and forums. Now it's often an AI tool that has read all of those. The need never changed. Only the messenger did.

## What changed for brands

The shelf keeps shrinking. A mall had hundreds of products. Google had ten blue links. An AI answer might mention three names.
