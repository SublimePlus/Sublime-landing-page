There's an old joke in SEO circles: the best place to hide a secret is page two of Google, because nobody ever goes there.

For years, that joke described the whole game. Get on page one or disappear. Climb from position nine to position three and watch the traffic roll in.

I think that era is ending. Not because Google is going away, but because more and more people never even reach the list of links. At Sublime Plus, we've started calling it the death of page two, and honestly, page one isn't looking too healthy either.

## The answer moved to the top

When Google shows an AI Overview, the answer sits right at the top of the page. For many questions, people read it and they're done.

[Pew Research Center looked at](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) real browsing data from March 2025 and found that people clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, compared with 15% when it didn't. They rarely clicked the links inside the summary either. Around one in five searches in that study produced an AI summary, and longer questions were more likely to trigger one.

So the click, the thing SEO was built around, is getting rarer.

## Questions are getting longer

People don't search like robots anymore. Instead of "running shoes flat feet," they type "what running shoes are good if I have flat feet and run on pavement." That's exactly the kind of question AI tools love to answer directly.

And outside Google, the same questions go to ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot, where there's no page of links at all. Just an answer.

## What replaces page one

If page two is dead and page one is shrinking, what's left is the answer itself.

The new goal is to be named inside it, whether that's an AI Overview, a ChatGPT reply or a Gemini recommendation. That's a different game from classic rankings. It depends a lot on what other people say about you across the web, not only on your own pages. I compared the two in [SEO vs LLM optimization](/blog/seo-vs-llm-optimization-what-s-the-difference).

## So is SEO dead?

No. Your website still matters a lot. AI Overviews pull from Google's index, and every AI tool needs a clear source of truth about who you are. Good SEO is the foundation.

But SEO alone used to be enough to get found. Now it's the price of entry. Getting chosen inside an AI answer takes something extra: a brand people actually talk about.

## What I'd watch closely

If I ran a brand today, I'd keep an eye on three things. How often an AI summary shows up for the questions my customers ask. Whether my brand appears inside those answers. And which sources the answers lean on, because those are the places worth being part of.

If competitors keep showing up and you don't, [your competitor shows up in ChatGPT and you don't](/blog/competitor-shows-up-in-chatgpt) explains the usual reasons.
