Type a simple question into ChatGPT: "What's a good coffee grinder for a beginner?" A few seconds later you have three or four suggestions, a sentence on each and maybe a tip about burr grinders versus blade grinders. It feels effortless.

But a lot happens behind that answer. On the Sublime marketing team, we find those few seconds fascinating, because they decide which brands a person ever hears about.

## First, it works out what you really mean

Before anything else, the AI tries to understand your intent. "Beginner" tells it you want something simple and forgiving. "Coffee grinder" sets the category. If you'd added "under $50" or "for a tiny apartment," the answer would shift completely.

This is why small words matter so much. Every detail in a question narrows the list of brands that can show up.

## Then it remembers, looks things up, or both

AI tools draw on two kinds of knowledge. One is what the model learned during training, a huge amount of text from across the internet. The other is live search, where tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity read fresh pages before answering.

For recommendation questions, live search is common. The tool might read a few review pages, a Reddit thread and a comparison article in the time it takes you to sip your coffee.

## Then it looks for patterns

The AI isn't picking favorites. It's looking for patterns. Which brands keep coming up? What are they praised for? What do people complain about?

If one grinder is mentioned again and again as "great for beginners," that phrase becomes part of the answer. Review sites play a big part here, which I wrote about in [the sites AI tools trust for brand mentions](/blog/review-sites-ai-tools-trust).

## Finally, it writes a short, confident answer

Then it hands you a short list, written in a friendly, confident tone. No page two. No ten tabs. Just a handful of names.

That confidence is a big reason people trust these answers. It feels like advice from a friend who did the homework.

## How many people do this now

More every month. [Pew Research Center found](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/the-share-of-americans-who-say-they-use-chatgpt-has-more-than-doubled-since-2023/) that in early 2026, 44% of US adults said they use ChatGPT or similar chatbots, up from 18% who had used ChatGPT in 2023. And on Google, a separate [Pew analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) found that around one in five searches in March 2025 already produced an AI summary at the top.

## Why the answer changes every time

Ask the same question twice and you might get different brands. Change one word and the list shifts. Ask Gemini instead of ChatGPT and it shifts again. AI answers aren't fixed rankings. They're fresh summaries every time.

## What this means if you sell something

If your brand isn't part of the patterns, it won't be part of the answer. And those patterns come mostly from what other people say about you, not from your own ads.
