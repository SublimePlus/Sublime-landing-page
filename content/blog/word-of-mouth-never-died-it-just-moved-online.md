Think about how your parents or grandparents chose a plumber. They didn't search anything. They asked the neighbor. A new car? They asked the cousin who "knows cars." A doctor? Whoever their best friend swore by.

That was word of mouth, and for most of history it was the most powerful marketing there was. Some people say it's fading. I think the opposite is true. Word of mouth never died. It just moved, and it got a lot louder.

At Sublime Plus Marketing, this is an idea we keep coming back to, so let me explain what I mean.

## The neighbor became a stranger with a username

Today, the "neighbor" might be someone you'll never meet. A person in a Reddit thread who owns the exact laptop you're considering. A reviewer on Google who ate at that restaurant last Tuesday. A stranger on a forum who had the same problem as you and found a fix.

We trust these people for the same reason we trusted the neighbor. They have nothing to gain from lying to us. Or at least, it feels that way.

## Reviews are word of mouth with a memory

An online review is word of mouth that stays put. One conversation used to help one person. One review can help thousands.

Researchers have been studying this for years. A whitepaper from [Northwestern University's Spiegel Research Center](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/04/Online-Reviews-Whitepaper.pdf) found that ratings and reviews rank among the biggest factors in what people buy, and that negative reviews can actually help build trust. People don't want perfect. They want real.

I wrote more about which review sites carry the most weight now in [Trustpilot, Yelp, G2 and the sites AI tools trust](/blog/review-sites-ai-tools-trust).

## AI is the friend who read everything

The newest twist is AI. When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini "what's a good running shoe for flat feet," they're asking a friend who has read every review and every Reddit thread on the topic.

That friend is getting popular. [Pew Research Center found](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/the-share-of-americans-who-say-they-use-chatgpt-has-more-than-doubled-since-2023/) that in early 2026, 44% of US adults said they use ChatGPT or similar chatbots, compared with 18% who had used ChatGPT in 2023.

So word of mouth now travels in three layers. People talk. Reviews and threads remember it. AI tools summarize it and pass it on. To understand that last layer, read [how ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity decide which brands to recommend](/blog/how-ai-tools-recommend-brands).

## What hasn't changed at all

The tools are new, but the rules are old.

Treat people well and they talk about you. Disappoint them and they talk louder. Do something genuinely surprising and they tell everyone they know.

No algorithm changes that. AI just makes it travel further and faster.

## What this means for your brand

If word of mouth moved online, your brand needs to be where the talking happens. That means giving people a reason to talk, making it easy for them to leave reviews, and being present in the communities where your customers ask each other for advice.

It also means paying attention to the conversation, because AI tools are listening to it whether you are or not.
