When a brand has a reputation problem, one of the first things people search is "online reputation management." They find a whole industry ready to help, often for a serious monthly fee.

Some of that industry does honest, useful work. Some of it relies on tactics that hold up for a while and then backfire. Improving how brands come across online is a big part of what we do at Sublime, so I want to explain the difference plainly, so you know what you're paying for.

## What traditional reputation management usually means

Classic online reputation management, or ORM, has focused on controlling what shows up when people search your name. Common tactics include creating lots of owned pages and profiles to push negative results lower on Google, requesting content removals, monitoring mentions and managing review profiles.

Some of this is perfectly reasonable. Monitoring and review management are useful. Building out your own profiles is fine.

The trouble starts when the whole strategy is about hiding things rather than changing them.

## Why hiding works less well now

Pushing a negative result to page two of Google used to solve most of the problem, because few people looked past page one.

AI tools changed that. ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews don't only read the top few results. They read widely and they summarize. A complaint thread you pushed down on Google can still show up when someone asks an AI tool about your brand. Suppression doesn't reach what these tools have already learned or can still find.

## What building real sentiment looks like

Building real sentiment means changing what people actually say about you, not just where it appears.

It starts with fixing the real problems behind the complaints. Then it means showing up honestly where conversations happen: replying to reviews, joining Reddit discussions as yourself, helping people with real answers. It means asking happy customers to share their experience. And it means keeping your information accurate everywhere, so AI tools describe you correctly.

It takes longer to show results than a suppression campaign. But the results last, because they're true.

## Red flags when hiring a reputation firm

Be careful with any firm that promises to remove every negative review, guarantees results within days, offers to post positive reviews for you, or won't explain exactly what it will do. Fake reviews and undisclosed paid posts can get your profiles penalized, and in the US the FTC takes fake reviews seriously.

Ask what they'll do each month, where they'll do it and how you'll see the work.
